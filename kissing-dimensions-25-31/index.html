New lower bounds for kissing numbers in dimensions 25, 26, 27, 29 and 31

Rustem Takhanov, Stanislav Yun

Paper & repository

Leech lifting in ℝ²⁴ ⊕ ℝᵏ · exact slice and certified data

Lifting the Leech shell, five times over

All five new bounds start from the 196,560 minimal vectors of the Leech lattice in ℝ²⁴. Some of them, the owners, are removed; points lifted out of ℝ²⁴ take their place, and an auxiliary configuration fills the extra coordinates. Each dimension then needs one local idea: a rotation, a coordinated motion, a deformed layer or a recoloured one.

Bulk175,904–194,989

Leech minimal vectors left in place, from d = 31 to d = 25.

Lifted points, first layers35.26°

19,840 (d = 29), 61,968 (d = 31); first cap layers of 4,440 (d = 26) and 6,774 (d = 27).

Caps, second layer30°

2,038 caps (x, ±½) in d = 25; 666 points in the recoloured second layer of d = 27.

Frame layer45°

480 points (d = 29) and 672 (d = 31). It removes no bulk point.

Auxiliary block90°

2 poles (d = 25), the hexagon (26), 12 (27), 34 (29), 122 (31).

Moved bulk3.22°

552 bulk points of d = 25 lifted slightly to make room for one more point.

Reading the drawings

  • Exact slice. The globe is the 3-space spanned by u and the tail plane, drawn to scale; caps have angular radius 30°.
  • Heights. Each angle is the arcsine of the length of the extra-coordinate part: 1/√3 gives 35.26°, ½ gives 30°, 1/√2 gives 45° and (√6 − 2)/8 gives 3.22°. The deformed second layer of dimension 26 has no single height.
  • Certificates. The angles in the slack figure were recomputed in floating point from the certificate files. The bounds themselves are certified in exact arithmetic by the authors’ verifiers.
